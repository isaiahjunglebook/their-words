## PART 1: WORLDVIEW — WHAT BRUNSON BELIEVES

**The customer is the hero; you are the guide.** (Via Donald Miller.) The business owner is Yoda, not Luke. Everything — funnels, emails, the Attractive Character — exists to guide dream customers through challenges to the result they want.

**A business is not products; it's results.** "People don't come to you because they want your product, they come to you because they want a specific result." Once both sides understand that, price stops being a barrier.

**The value ladder IS the business plan.** "If someone doesn't have a value ladder, they don't really have a business; they are just selling a product." Humans naturally seek more value from places they've already received it (the dating → marriage progression) — the ladder is how you honor that instinct commercially.

**The deepest funnel wins.** Dan Kennedy's law, quoted throughout: "Ultimately, the business that can spend the most to acquire a customer wins." Depth of monetization — upsells, bumps, ascension — is what lets you outbid everyone for attention. This is how Brunson survived the Google slap when every shallow competitor died.

**Never compete on price.** Dan Kennedy again: "There is no strategic advantage of being the second lowest price leader in town, but there is a huge strategic advantage of being the most expensive." Two ways to be the cheapest in your market: cut price (death), or raise perceived value so high your price seems trivial (the $750k iPhone auction).

**A confused mind always says no.** The fundamental law behind funnels-over-websites. A website is a brochure-waving salesperson; a funnel is your best closer cloned, walking one person to one action per page.

**Everything is Hook, Story, Offer.** The master lens, discovered late (not in the 2015 edition). Every ad, page, email, webinar, and phone call has all three. "If something isn't working in your funnel, it's always either the hook, the story, or the offer." A $100k consulting day reduces to this sentence.

**Model, never copy.** Pioneers have arrows in their backs (Porter Stansberry / Agora). Success = find who already succeeded with your market or funnel type and model the framework — your product, your words, your art inside their proven structure. Copying is illegal and lazy; modeling is how Brunson won at wrestling and business alike.

**A buyer is a buyer is a buyer.** (Dan Kennedy.) Someone who buys once will buy again if you keep providing value. Segment the world into subscribers, buyers, and hyperactive buyers, and treat each differently.

**Buyers buy in windows of pain.** The bowling story: hyperactive buying happens in the moment of pain; days later the window closes forever. If you don't offer the upsell now, a competitor gets the money — offering more is service, not greed, IF your product is genuinely the best answer.

**You own nothing but your list.** Traffic you control (paid) and traffic you earn (PR, social) are rented. The only goal for both is conversion into traffic you own. The list is the raise you give yourself daily: ~$1 per name per month as the floor.

**The market is the only judge.** "I don't even trust my own opinion." Split tests, not opinions. Jim Edwards's corollary: every headline you fall in love with fails; the winners are about THEM, not you.

**Fundamentals beat flash.** Wrestling-camp moves lose matches; single-legs and double-legs win state titles. 95% of Brunson's revenue comes from three simple funnel types. Complexity is a consultant's vanity.

**You're just one funnel away.** One good funnel, properly exploited (via Gary Halbert's beach-idea line), is worth more than ten lifetimes of hard work. This is the emotional core of the doctrine: the game is winnable, the next attempt might be the one.

## PART 2: FRAMEWORKS (Complete Inventory)

### 2.1 The Secret Formula (Secret #1)
The four questions every business must answer, in order — Brunson's $100k/day consulting structure:

1. **WHO is your dream customer?** Choose consciously or wake up serving people who exhaust you. Method: write avatars (Brunson's "Julie" and "Mike"), list characteristics, find a literal photo, put it on the wall. Six years after doing this, Brunson's $50k/seat room of 100 top clients matched the avatars almost person-for-person. JLD's "Jimmy" avatar drives every Entrepreneur on Fire decision ("WWJD"). Diagnostic proof: the PCOS makeup-brush company (Dean & Robyn Holland) went $34k/year → $100k/60 days by changing nothing except WHO the pages spoke to.
2. **WHERE are they congregating?** The internet's power is congregations (TheMat.com for wrestlers; Magic: The Gathering forums). Locate existing gatherings: websites, forums, FB groups, influencers, podcasts, newsletters, blogs, YouTube channels, search keywords. Marketing = put bait where the congregation already is.
3. **What BAIT (hook, story, offer) attracts them?** Bait must attract dream customers and repel wrong ones (the "108 Split Test Winners" offer attracted Mikes/Julies, filtered beginners). "If you change your bait, you'll change your customer."
4. **What unique RESULT do you create for them?** The pinnacle: what would you do if they could pay anything? (Brunson's answer: $1M + equity, done-for-you.) You work backward from that summit.

**Decision rule:** every business problem routes through these four questions first; #3 expands into Hook-Story-Offer, #4 expands into the Value Ladder.

### 2.2 Hook, Story, Offer (Secret #2) — THE master framework
Every step of every funnel — ads, emails, landing pages, upsells, webinars, phone calls — contains all three. **If something isn't working, it's always the hook, the story, or the offer.**

Build order is reversed from the name — offer first:

**OFFER.** Almost everyone starts selling a commodity (physical, info, or service that can be bought elsewhere). The escape is restructuring the product into an offer whose goals are: (a) increase perceived value, (b) make it unique to you, available only inside this offer. Method: whiteboard brainstorm — "What else could we give them that would guarantee their success?" — write everything, strike the nonnegotiables, stack the rest. **The 10x rule: total offer value must be ≥10x price.** Price resistance means insufficient value, not poor customers ($10k shoes vs. $10k Ferrari). Kiana the 12-year-old babysitter: commodity $5/hr → stacked offer (dishes, activities, photos collage, treats, kids asleep) → Brunson would pay $20/hr and book weeks ahead. Offer creation is a permanent, repeating role — Claude Hopkins was paid $52k in 1907 (~$1.4M today) as a "scheme man" inventing offers.

**STORY.** The story increases the perceived value of the offer. Drew Manning's fit-to-fat-to-fit journey is what made "Keto Jumpstart" irresistible → Two Comma Club ($1M), then Two Comma Club X ($10M). One story per funnel step. Deep story structure (belief-breaking) is explicitly deferred to Expert Secrets.

**HOOK.** Headlines, images, videos thrown where dream customers congregate to grab attention long enough to tell the story. Hooks carry no value themselves. Each story has DOZENS of potential hooks; you never know which lands, so keep throwing until one grabs (Drew: Jay Leno clip, before/after pics, food shots, weight-gain headlines...).

### 2.3 The Value Ladder (Secret #3)
Ascending offers, value and price rising together: **bait (free) → frontend ($1-100) → middle ($100-2k) → backend ($2k+) → continuity throughout.** The dentist parable: free cleaning postcard → $2,000 walk-out (whitening + retainers) → cosmetic surgery tier ($10k) → 6-month recall continuity.

**Value Ladder Mission Statement (VLMS):** "We help (who) to (result) through (new opportunity)." ClickFunnels: "We help visionary entrepreneurs grow their companies through sales funnels." Every offer at every rung fulfills the same VLMS by different means (Stacey & Paul Martino's "yellow brick road to their Oz").

**Structural rules:**
- Most businesses have only a fragment of a ladder; the fix is adding front-end (sexier bait — Dr. Woolner's boring "free exam" → massage + supplements + meditation CD, clinic filled in months, $5k wellness backend) and back-end (there is NO ceiling: the $25k Bill Glazer/Dan Kennedy mastermind upsold 9 of 18 members into a $30k movie).
- Boring front-end offer = business always struggles.
- If stuck for rungs: custom-suit maker added free cufflinks as bait; bundles, coaching, events for backend.
- Each rung is sold by a different funnel class (see 2.8).

**The one-funnel focus rule:** sketch the whole ladder, then build ONE funnel — usually middle/back where price covers ads — and you are forbidden from building tier two until that funnel hits the Two Comma Club ($1M gross). Liz Benny: $1k course webinar → $1M in a year → THEN the high-ticket coaching tier filled in days from her own list. New front-end offers get built only when ads for the core offer fatigue.

### 2.4 The Attractive Character (Secret #4)
The brand personality that binds people to the value ladder: "they come for the result, they stay because of their relationship with you." Jenna Kutcher: "A brand is the image and personality the business applies to its offers."

**Four elements:**
1. **Backstory — shared often.** Nobody follows you until they know you walked their path (Dr. Woolner's real origin: wife's car accident → chiropractor healed her → changed majors). You'll tire of your backstory long before the market does.
2. **Parables.** Teach every concept through story (Mark Schultz emptying Russell's wallet for the "Total Violence" tape = the investment parable). Build a parable Rolodex.
3. **Flaws.** Perfect characters alienate (Superman needs kryptonite; Stan Lee's flawed humans beat DC's gods). Vulnerability creates relatability.
4. **Polarity.** Neutral is invisible. Take stands, split the audience into fans/neutrals/haters (Howard Stern). Haters are the price of raving fans who buy.

**Four identities (pick one):** Leader (walked the path, leads others down it) / Adventurer (goes out, brings back treasure) / Reporter (interviews experts, borrows status — Brunson's starting identity) / Reluctant Hero (duty compels sharing despite shyness — Brunson's current identity).

**Six storylines:** loss & redemption; us vs. them; before & after; amazing discovery; secret-telling (the trilogy's own branding); third-person testimonial.

Deep mastery (belief-breaking through story) deferred to Expert Secrets.

### 2.5 Funnel Hacking (Secret #5)
Model, don't copy. Before building ANY funnel, find others already succeeding with your market or funnel type — if nobody is, don't proceed. Sources: direct competitors, indirect competitors (same demo, different product), same-funnel-type sellers in any market. Process: act as a customer, buy through 6+ funnels, and at every ad/page log the hook, the story, the offer. Wear the consultant hat: what would you improve? This sets the baseline your hooks/stories/offers must beat. Armand Morin's software funnel: Brunson modeled structure, layout, and pricing strategy — own product, own words, own art.

### 2.6 Seven Phases of a Funnel (Secret #6)
The pre-frame principle governs everything: each step is the pre-frame for the next. MIT warm/cold bio study (Sway): one adjective flipped an identical lecture's ratings. Mark Joyner: "not all clicks are created equal" — the frame someone passes through often matters more than the page itself. Armand Morin's introduction took Brunson's stage close from 15% → 42%; a controlled video intro kept him >40% forever.

1. **Determine traffic temperature.** Hot (knows you), warm (knows solutions, not you), cold (knows only pain). Maps to Eugene Schwartz's awareness continuum: product-aware → desire-aware → problem-aware. Each temperature needs its own bridge and often its own landing page.
2. **Pre-frame bridge.** Hot = quick email. Warm = short video giving context. Cold = full bridge page that educates vocabulary first (nerve pain → "that's neuropathy" → landing page now makes sense). Cold bridges expand your universe exponentially.
3. **Qualify subscribers.** Squeeze page; those unwilling to give an email won't give money. (~30% baseline.)
4. **Qualify buyers — IMMEDIATELY.** "A buyer is a buyer is a buyer" (Kennedy). Free-plus-shipping or $7-10 offer straight after opt-in. Now you hold two lists (subscribers, buyers) treated differently.
5. **Identify hyperactive buyers.** The bowling story: pain-window purchases. Give them upsells NOW or competitors collect. Sublist them; spend more marketing to a $1,000 buyer than a $10 one.
6. **Age & ascend.** Phases 1-5 happen in the 5-10 minute point of sale; from here follow-up funnels + the Attractive Character move them up the ladder.
7. **Change the selling environment.** >$2k rarely sells from a webpage; move to phone/direct mail/live events where live feedback closes.

Stuck business = one of these seven phases is broken; audit in order.

### 2.7 Follow-Up Funnels (Secret #7)
**Three types of traffic:** control (you pay — ads, solos, affiliates), earn (PR, social, SEO, word of mouth), own (your lists). The ONLY goal of the first two is conversion into the third. Controlled traffic → squeeze page; earned traffic → funnel hub (website-looking page whose every path enters a funnel). List math: ~$1/name/month minimum with a good relationship (Brunson: 200 names/$200 first month → 1M+ names in five years).

**Soap Opera Sequence (SOS)** — 5 emails, one/day, immediately after opt-in, sequence-based. Goal: fast bond with the Attractive Character + pull into the current funnel. Structure (via Andre Chaperon; start-at-drama via Daegan Smith):
1. Set the stage + first hook/open loop.
2. High drama opening → backstory → hit the wall (where the reader is now) → promise the answer tomorrow.
3. Epiphany — the discovery, tied to the One Thing you sell.
4. Hidden benefits they haven't considered.
5. Urgency + CTA (real scarcity only — fake urgency destroys credibility).
Style: 1-2 sentence lines, white space, skimmable. Chain: finish one SOS → enter the next funnel's SOS, or graduate to Seinfeld list.

**Daily Seinfeld Emails** — broadcast (not sequence), daily, to everyone not in an active SOS. 90% entertainment / 10% content ("emails about nothing"). Structure = Hook (subject), Story (entertainment), Offer (tie back to something you sell — EVERY email sells something or you're the world's best-paid unpaid entertainer). Three styles: Episode (today's life event), Epiphany (idea/challenge belief), Educational (list/how-to/FAQ). Ben Settle: one $97/mo newsletter, daily emails, ten years. Frequency conviction: monthly = horrible; each frequency doubling grew income; daily = every skipped day loses money. Two documented emails ($20M toilet flush; jiu-jitsu "wrestling for old fat guys") each made $100k+.

### 2.8 The Ten Funnels (Section Two) — by value-ladder tier

**FREE tier — Lead Funnels:**
- **Lead "Squeeze" (Secret #8):** squeeze page (curiosity headline = hook; lead magnet = offer; story minimal) → thank-you page (deliver magnet + point to next funnel). Origin: pop-ups → pop-up-as-page (Eben Pagan's Kiss Test). Variant: reverse squeeze (video first) — fewer but better-qualified leads.
- **Survey (Secret #9):** survey page (one hidden "bucket question" determines routing — Ryan Levesque's Ask) → per-bucket squeeze with niche case study → per-bucket results page with tailored message. Works when many different people need the same product differently (FuzzyYellowBalls "Serve Killer" quiz; ClickFunnels' business-type survey). Results template: "Based on your answers, your #1 reason for [pain] is likely because you don't have [surprise], which typically costs [amount] every [period]."
- **Summit (Secret #10, Bailey Richert guest chapter):** registration page (headline + all 30 speaker faces) → special offer page (~$100 or less all-access pass: lifetime recordings + bonuses; OR a challenge offer with recordings as bonus) → broadcast pages (24hr windows/day, 3-4 days, then videos vanish). Leverages 30 speakers' lists via affiliate commissions; zero-ad-spend list building + borrowed authority. 30 Days Summit: $1M in under two weeks.

**$1-100 tier — Unboxing Funnels.** Concept: take a big offer (e.g., $997 / Brunson's $840 Expert Secrets stack), unbox it, sell one component per page. Shared machinery:
- **100 Visitor Test:** $197 straight sale = 1% conversion = $197/100 visitors, 1 buyer. Free-plus-shipping splinter + same $197 as upsell = 8% front conversion, 25% upsell take = $394/100 visitors, 8 buyers. Double money, 8x buyers. "Sell + free shipping" converts the same or better than free-plus-shipping if you dislike "free."
- **First-yes psychology:** card already out → friction gone for yes #2 (one-click upsells).
- **Order form bump:** checkbox after card info, before submit. Grocery-lane impulse buy; must need zero explanation ("add the audiobook for $27?"). ~33% take rate, no LTV damage, typically covers all ad costs so upsells are pure profit.
- **CPA vs ACV:** the two numbers that rule funnels (full math in 2.10).
- **Book Funnel (Secret #11):** for ANY free-plus-shipping info product (books, CDs, DVDs, preloaded MP3 players, one-page scripts). Sales page: curiosity headline; left = story video ("Who,What,Why,How" or "Star,Story,Solution"); right = **two-step order form** (step 1 shipping — converts higher than a squeeze page because physical mail has higher perceived value; step 2 card + bump; disclose S&H on step 1 or it's unethical). Page blocks top-to-bottom: emotion → logic (bullets) → fear (urgency/scarcity). $7-27 digital products: order form at bottom, build value first. Upsells: **OTO rule for info products = NEVER more of the same** (their problem feels solved at purchase; sell the next logical need — funnels book → funnel software/traffic course). No Thanks link: adjacent to button, negatively framed ("No thanks, I don't want this amazing bonus at 95% off"). Downsell options: digital-only version or payment plan. Max two upsells for info or LTV drops. Thank-you page → next funnel (Dean Graziosi: book → webinar registration).
- **Cart Funnel (Secret #12):** e-commerce. Demo video sells physical products (Billy Mays; Harmon Brothers' Squatty Potty unicorn / FiberFix). Front-end = sexiest best-selling SKU restructured as a bundle: best-seller + lesser SKUs FREE ("buy 3 one-inch wraps, get two 2-inch FREE and one 4-inch FREE") — headline states the offer plainly (less curiosity than info products). Order form: quantity selector + bump (rush shipping, complementary SKU) = two ACV levers on one page. **OTO rule for physical = DO sell more of the same at a discount** (buyers stock up/gift), then next-logical SKUs in best-seller order. 3-4 upsells acceptable (Amazon-conditioned buyers). Thank-you page = **offer wall** (Trey Lewellen's flashlight → targets, oil, more).
- **Challenge Funnel (Secret #13, Natasha Hazlett guest chapter):** THE value-ladder doorway. Sales page essentials: **a clear hook — simple, believable promised result** (Cristy "CodeRed" Nickel: "Lose 10 lbs in 30 days"); short AC story; the path; **prizes that seed the next offer up the ladder**; the Stack; screenshots-as-testimonials; bump that enhances challenge completion (audiobook ~30%+ take). Design: 14-30 days ($47 typical), closed FB group, LIVE videos (urgency, community; prerecord-streaming via OneStream et al. acceptable). Upsells = completion aids only (accountability coach; journal+workbook kit) — never more content to consume. Endgame webinar economics: direct webinar ads = 18% show, 4% close; post-challenge webinar = 70% register, 50%+ show, 20%+ close. $47 challenger LTV >$420. Success/failure = clarity of promise + believability + expert credibility. ClickFunnels' One Funnel Away = 5,000 first run, woven into every funnel's flow.

**$100-2,000 tier — Presentation Funnels.** Higher price needs a real sales presentation to raise perceived value; all three use the Perfect Webinar architecture (mastery deferred to Expert Secrets).
- **VSL Funnel (Secret #14):** 20-30 min presentation, <$100 products (or memberships/continuity). Like book/cart page but order form NEVER at top — value before price. Video spoiler box ("In Today's Free Presentation" + 4 blocks syncing to the three secrets + CTA hint) pushes people back into the video. CTA section visible-throughout vs. appear-at-CTA: test both, mixed results.
- **Webinar Funnel (Secret #15):** $100-2,000. Pages: registration (pure curiosity — "My Weird Niche Funnel Making $17,947/Day," date/time, one button, email pop-up → SOS) → thank-you (Who,What,Why,How video; optional low-ticket offer that recoups ad spend — must complement, never cannibalize the webinar offer) → webinar room (GoToWebinar/Zoom; ClickFunnels builds pages, not the room) → order form (RECAP the offer, never resell — reselling on the order form kills sales) → replay page (48-72hr countdown, then offline; ~80% won't attend live, emails push replay). Bumps/upsells/offer walls all pluggable. **Automate ONLY after mastering live** — Brunson did 1-3 live/day for a year first. The 10X story: same 90-min script, 9,000 people, $3.2M — and the identical presentation runs 30,000x/month automated.
- **Product Launch Funnel (Secret #16):** Jeff Walker's "sideways sales letter" — the webinar tipped on its side across days/weeks. Best for warm/hot traffic (own list, JV lists), new-product anticipation (Hollywood trailer model). Pages: free-workshop registration (squeeze; workshop IS the magnet) → video pages 1-3 (thumbnails w/ locks, comments for social proof, lead magnets beneath) → offer page (video 4 + order form). Automate after the live run.

**$2,000+ tier — Back-End Phone Funnels:**
- **Application Funnel (Secret #17):** change the selling environment. History: Matt Bacak's $4.95 CD → 1-in-10 closed into $8k workshop; Brunson's Christmas rescue (800 DVDs → phones → 11 × $5,500); then the 60-salesperson call center at ~$1M/month overhead, shut down; replaced by applications + TWO closers = same gross, ~10x net. Pages: (1) success-story or case-study page — show WHAT/strategy, never HOW/tactics; the withheld how compels application; collect email for follow-up; (2) application page — the **takeaway sale: they sell YOU**. Video + testimonial rush playing while they type. Key questions: disguised revenue qualifiers ("How much do you spend on ads monthly?"), the Dan Sullivan three-year question (success picture — misaligned visions like "$10M in 12 months, no product" = decline), good-fit-for-group, contribution-to-group (attract givers, repel takers); (3) homework page — AC story video (connection; people call in tears), expectations + prep homework, and **get THEM to call you: inbound applicants are worth 4x** outbound calls.

### 2.9 The Funnel Scripts (Section Three)
- **Curiosity-Based Headlines (Secret #18, Jim Edwards guest chapter):** the headline's job is to hook and pre-frame, NEVER to sell. No headline = content invisible (the duct-taped LA Times demo). Templates per temperature — hot names product, warm names benefit/desire, cold names problem (Schwartz mapping). Question and pure-curiosity templates for all temps. Double clicks ≠ double profit — it can be 10x+ profit (the $900-ads/$1,000-product math: 2x clicks → $100 profit becomes $1,100). "Trailer Trash to Piles of Cash" (about him) = zero sales; "Unfair Advantage in Business and Life" (about them, self-completing curiosity) = 5 sales in 5 minutes. Rules: headline about THEM; headlines you fall in love with fail; the only opinion is the credit card's; test constantly — scripts are the flashlight in the gold mine.
- **Who, What, Why, How (Secret #19):** the workhorse for ads, squeeze videos, low-ticket sales, webinar thank-yous. WHO am I → WHAT do I have (hook + offer) → WHY do you need it (**PAS: Problem, Agitate, Solve**) → HOW to get it (CTA). Amplifiers: the Catch (name it — people assume one anyway), real urgency/scarcity, guarantee (risk reversal), recap.
- **Star, Story, Solution (Secret #20):** 20-30 min VSL script, <$100 products. Origin: Vince James ($100M in 23 months). **STAR:** pattern interrupt/hook (big promise, open loop) → core-desire questions (yes-momentum) → agitate past failures ("this isn't the first time... when is it your turn?") → big promise/One Thing → introduce the star. **STORY:** start at HIGH DRAMA (never the beginning; the spam-complaint phone call), → backstory → the wall (= where the reader is) → identify the problem → epiphany/Declaration of Independence → the path (things tried and failed) → first sign of success → conspiracy ("it wasn't my fault") → the Big Lie ("for years they told me ___") → common enemy → rapid growth → case studies → hidden benefits. **SOLUTION:** formal intro ("That's why I created ___") → pain & cost to build → ease → speed → "so you can" benefits (feature + so-you-can + benefit, always) → social proof → offer + Stack → 10x price anchor → emotional "If all" close (yes ladder) → reveal real price (drop sequence) → logic guarantee → real urgency/scarcity (fear close) → future pace → CTA → post-sell → takeaway warning ("doesn't matter to us if you join") → recap for skimmers.
- **OTO Script (Secret #21):** 3-5 min, $27-997. Tim Erway's cardinal-mistake diagnosis: reselling the buyer. They already said yes; get the second yes. Sequence: confirm decision + **keep the loop open** ("WAIT! Your order is not yet complete" — "congratulations, order complete" kills conversions) → smart-why ("you made a smart choice, here's why") → question (info: "Now that you have X, people ask me about Y"; physical: "want more at a discount?") → exclusive (action-takers only) → results-faster → **the One Thing** (never explain everything — one killer component, e.g., the Stack video out of 24 hours of content) → future pace → CTA → guarantee → value-stack FREE × n → one-time scarcity ("when you leave this page, it's gone") → second CTA → testimonial rush. Reworked across a dozen funnels: near-zero → double-digit conversions overnight.
- **Perfect Webinar (Secret #22):** the trilogy's crown script (mastery = Expert Secrets; Jaime Cross's 5-minute e-com version exists). 90 min = 15 intro / 45 content / 30 Stack & Close; ratios hold at any length. **INTRO:** big promise (the ruler they'll judge you with) → hook to end (stay-to-the-finish giveaway) → command attention (close Facebook) → qualify yourself (story) → future pace. **CONTENT:** the One Thing / big domino ("funnels are the #1 way to grow a company" — if they believe it, money follows) taught via personal origin story; teach the WHAT (vehicle/framework), never the HOW (that's the product); then **Three Secrets** breaking the three false-belief classes: the vehicle, internal ("I can't"), external ("something outside me blocks it") — each broken and rebuilt through story (High-Ticket Secrets example: vehicle "info products are the best model" → "one high-ticket day beats a month"; internal "I must sell by phone" → "two-person mini call center"; external "traffic costs a fortune" → "~100 clicks/day"). **STACK & CLOSE:** transition "Let me ask you a question... is it okay if I spend 10 minutes on a special offer?" (moral obligation to offer) → **the Stack** (Armand Morin): prospects only remember the LAST thing shown, so every new element re-lists everything before it, ending with the full Stack slide + total value — took Brunson from 5-10% closes to 30-50%+ → "If all" yes-ladder (toward pleasure, away from pain, 3-5 yeses) → price-drop reveal ($11,185 → $4,997 retail → not even $1,997 → today's price) → CTA.
- **Product Launch Script (Secret #23):** the webinar split in four. V1 = Intro + Secret 1; V2 = Secret 2; V3 = Secret 3; V4 = Stack & Close. **V1 "Wow & How":** introduce the NEW OPPORTUNITY (new vehicle, never an improvement offer — deferred depth in Expert Secrets) → kill false beliefs via origin story → others' results → hook to V2 (comments for engagement). **V2 "Transformational Education":** over-the-shoulder demo (they see themselves doing it) → your framework steps (the what) → framework proof (one case study walked through the steps) → hook. **V3 "External Forces":** future pace → name external roadblocks/false beliefs, break via stories → hint the offer → hook to V4. **V4 "The Offer":** Here's what I've got (Stack) → what you'll do with it (ownership experience, show the buying process) → what it'll do for you (logic benefits) → what to do right now (CTA + urgency/scarcity, consequences of waiting).
- **Four-Question Close (Secret #24):** solo expert phone close, $2k-8k+. Frame control opener: "I'm going to ask you four questions. Depending on how you answer and how well we get along, we'll decide whether to move forward. Sound fair?" (micro-yes + ground rules). **Q1** (Dan Sullivan): coffee shop a year from now — what happened for you to feel happy? Dig external desires ($10k/mo, house) down to internal (respect, inclusion, purpose) with why-questions; can't articulate = don't work with them. **Q2:** why don't you have it yet? Listen for "I don't know how" (workable) vs. blame of others (disqualify — no responsibility, no results). **Q3:** what unused resources, talents, connections, skills could we leverage? "What else? What else?" until empty → recap summary (want / blocked-by / unleveraged) → "how much more would you make if we eliminated the obstacles and leveraged those resources?" — they state the value. **Q4:** "Do you want me to help you achieve your goals?" then SILENCE. Yes → "My fee is $___. I can transfer you to my assistant right now." Objections at this point are money-only → payment plan.
- **Setter & Closer Scripts (Secret #25):** two-person team, $2k-100k. **SETTER:** low-key intro → emotion mining (dreams + why-behind-why: kids' names, feelings; "people buy on emotion, rationalize with logic") → "what's holding you back?" (surface "I don't know how" in some form) → "If you knew how, would you do it?" (they begin selling themselves) → blast/taste of the program → the pivotal question: "If you could work one-on-one with [expert], would you succeed? ...How come?" then SHUT UP (their reasons are truth; yours might be a lie) → repeat/confirm beliefs → "why would you be a good candidate?" → posture ("I'm just the screener; the director decides; don't be offended if there's no offer") → permission to probe → financials (all decision-makers on the phone or stop; credit questions: rating, total debt, credit-card debt, total credit extended → available credit vs. program cost; savings/home/retirement; OPM concept as short-term leverage) → short-term goals (6mo/12mo, "can you see yourself achieving them? how come?") → **four commitments** (time/week; coachable — "why are you coachable?"; start today; investment level — "which of the two tiers are you comfortable with and why?") → homework (WRITE 6- and 12-month financial goals + three non-money wants) → handoff with director name written down. **CLOSER:** re-ask the dream questions (reinforce; connect dreams to program: "how would working with [expert] make a difference? ...anything else?") → four commitments as explicit yes/no gates (time; decision-making today; resources — "write this number down: $___; as long as the value's there, any reason you couldn't invest this today?"; teachability — "how come? so if someone showed you how, you'd succeed?") → what's included (list) → the close: **"Why do you feel like you are a good candidate for this program?"** — they sell themselves a final time and effectively ask YOU to let them buy → take the card.

### 2.10 Funnel Math, Selection, Stacking, Audibles (Section Four)
**Funnel selection by price:** $0 → lead funnels; $1-100 → unboxing; $100-2,000 → presentation; $2,000+ → phone. Cross-tier moves are legal (unbox a $1k offer; sell $100 off a lead-funnel thank-you). Sketch first: pages, bump?, how many upsells?, downsell?, and per page: hook, story, offer, price. ClickFunnels mechanics: sections (green) > rows (blue) > elements (orange); Funnel Wizard; share-funnel templates for all 10 at DotComSecrets.com/resources. The pages are scaffolding — the money is the hooks, stories, offers you plug in.

**CPA & ACV:** CPA = ad spend ÷ customers. ACV = total funnel sales ÷ customers. **CPA < ACV = working funnel: spend as much as possible ("a slot machine that pays $100 per $20"). CPA > ACV = broken: call audibles.** Per-ad CPA too: kill losers fast, ride winners till fatigue (online ads fatigue in weeks, not TV's 18 months — Dean Graziosi outsold Brunson 4:1 by making 4x the ad creative on his phone).

**Funnel stacking:** one funnel per ladder rung, each funnel's exit feeding the next funnel's entrance (thank-you pages, offer walls, challenge graduation webinars) plus follow-up funnels for slow movers. 95% of Brunson's revenue = three funnels (book, webinar, high-ticket). Fast movers must be offered the next step NOW or they buy elsewhere; slow movers took 3-4 years then $25k in a week. NEVER wait to launch until the stack is complete — Two Comma Club in funnel one, then build funnel two and retrofit the link.

**Funnel audibles:** launch expecting failure ("most funnels fail the first time — mine all do"); the launch is the first wrestling match (state-title story), pure data collection. Test budget = the revenue of ONE full-funnel buyer ($1,000 webinar → $1,000 ads; book funnel $0+$37+$97+$197 → $331). Goal: break even = free lead machine. Read the sheet: CPA on the left; per page, price × conversion% = contribution; sum = ACV; ACV - CPA = score. Then fix pages below benchmark by asking, in order, page by page: what's the hook? the story? the offer? — brainstorm, split-test one change at a time, winner becomes the new control, repeat ("give yourself a raise every day": 3%→4% on a $1k/week page = +$17,362/yr). Diagnosis heuristics: high CPA = usually the ad (hooks), sometimes landing page; low show-up + high reg cost = headline gives away the answer (Schmidt/Rivera: curiosity rewrite took registration from $24.85→$5.84, show-up 22.4%→31.7%). The market is the only judge; don't trust your own opinion; 3-4 test cycles typically reach a winner. Complexity kills diagnosis — the 50-page if/then funnel map was unfixable BECAUSE it was complex; simple 3-5 page funnels have few levers, so you find the broken one fast.

**Conversion benchmarks (below = audible):** lead funnel opt-in >20%; two-step order form step-1 >10-15%; book/cart sales >1-5%; bump >20%; OTO/downsell >3-15%; VSL sales >1-3%; webinar registration >20%; webinar show-up live >10-20%, automated >50-80%; webinar buy live-hot >10%, live-cold >1-5%, automated >3-8%; PLF registration >20%; cost per application <$100.

## PART 3: REASONING PATTERNS — HOW BRUNSON THINKS

**Pattern 1: Framework extraction through repetition.** Brunson doesn't invent from theory; he funnel-hacks dozens of instances, transcribes them, doodles the shape, and names the pattern (that's how every script in Section Three was born). When facing a new problem, his first move is "who has already solved this, and what's the common structure?"

**Pattern 2: Diagnose upstream before downstream.** FitLife.tv "didn't have a traffic or conversion problem — they had a value ladder problem." The presenting symptom is rarely the disease. Order of suspicion: dream customer → offer (commodity?) → value ladder completeness → funnel type match → page-level hook/story/offer → traffic.

**Pattern 3: Unit economics as the arbiter.** Every strategic question collapses to CPA vs ACV. Should you add an upsell? Will it raise ACV without hurting LTV? Should you run that ad? Only while its CPA < ACV. He doesn't argue taste; he arranges tests and reads the credit-card vote.

**Pattern 4: Psychology drives structure.** Every structural choice has a stated psychological mechanism: two-step order form (commitment begun), bump placement after card info (purchase already happened in the mind), open loops in OTOs ("order not yet complete"), the Stack (recency memory), takeaway selling (they chase what might be withheld), pre-frames (MIT study). When evaluating any funnel change, ask what it does to the buyer's internal state.

**Pattern 5: Constraint as forcing function.** One funnel until Two Comma Club. Two upsells max for info. One call to action per page. 14-30 day challenges, never longer. Five-email SOS. Brunson's power moves are subtractions; he treats optionality as the enemy of diagnosis and speed.

**Pattern 6: Serve harder = sell more, and mean it.** The resolution of the sales-vs-service tension is always "more value": upsells exist because buyers in pain deserve complete solutions; daily emails are fine because they entertain; the million-dollar tier exists because some people genuinely want that depth. If a recommendation doesn't serve the customer at a higher level, he doesn't trust it to convert long-term.

**Pattern 7: Speed to data over polish.** "Money follows speed" (Vitale). Launch the simple version, spend one buyer's worth of ads, read numbers, adjust. Weeks-long build cycles are a failure mode; the old three-month hand-coded funnels were the pain that birthed ClickFunnels.

**Pattern 8: Identity and story before tactics.** When someone's business feels dead (Dr. Woolner's "it paid well and I got Fridays off"), Brunson digs for the true backstory first, because the Attractive Character powers every page. Tactic questions from someone with no defined dream customer get bounced back to the Secret Formula.

## PART 4: DECISION LAYER

### Master Diagnostic Sequence (run in order; stop at first failure)
1. **Dream customer defined?** No avatar with a face and characteristics → Secret Formula Q1 before anything else. Symptom: exhausting clients, unfocused messaging, "selling to everyone."
2. **Congregations located?** Can't name 5+ specific places they gather → Q2. Symptom: "I don't know where to advertise."
3. **Offer or commodity?** If what they sell can be bought elsewhere and isn't 10x-value stacked → build the offer first (whiteboard method). Symptom: price resistance, margin compression, "my market won't pay."
4. **Value ladder complete?** Missing sexy front-end or any back-end → add rungs (front-end fixes acquisition; back-end fixes profitability). Symptom: "traffic/conversion problem" that's really a ladder problem; nothing to sell after first purchase.
5. **One funnel, right type, right tier?** Match price to funnel class ($0 lead / $1-100 unboxing / $100-2k presentation / $2k+ phone). Multiple half-built funnels → collapse to one until $1M.
6. **Funnel-hacked baseline?** No models studied → buy through 6+ before building. Nobody succeeding in the market at all → do not proceed.
7. **Launched with test math?** Not launched → launch this week with one-buyer's-revenue ad budget. Launched and losing → audible sequence: find below-benchmark page → hook? story? offer? → one-variable split test → repeat.
8. **List being built and mailed?** No SOS on opt-in, no daily Seinfeld → follow-up funnels are the multiplier on everything above ($1/name/month floor).
9. **Ascension paths live?** Funnel exits dead-ending → stack funnels (thank-you pages, offer walls, graduation webinars). Fast movers with nowhere to go = money handed to competitors.

### If-then rules (canonical)
- IF CPA < ACV → scale spend aggressively; the funnel is a slot machine paying out.
- IF CPA > ACV → stop scaling; run audibles; the market said no (to a page, not to you).
- IF ad CPA rises toward ACV → ad fatigue; replace creative (weeks-scale online), don't touch the funnel yet.
- IF a page underperforms benchmark → it is the hook, the story, or the offer; test in that order of suspicion (hook first — cheapest, most common).
- IF webinar registration is expensive AND show-up is low → headline reveals the answer; rewrite for curiosity (tell them what it's NOT about).
- IF selling info and designing OTO → next logical need, never more of the same.
- IF selling physical and designing OTO → more of the same at a discount first, then next-logical SKUs.
- IF price > ~$2,000 → change the selling environment (application funnel + phones); don't fight the Add-to-Cart ceiling.
- IF price $100-2,000 → presentation funnel (VSL <$100 / webinar / PLF for launches to warm lists).
- IF offer is big but you want front-end customers → unbox it instead of presenting it.
- IF new to webinars → live, 1+ per day, until the presentation is mastered; only then automate.
- IF the buyer just said yes → the next offer belongs NOW (hyperactive window), with the loop held open.
- IF a lead applies for high-ticket → make them call you (4x value), make them sell you (takeaway), disguise the money question.
- IF a prospect blames others for past failures (Q2 / setter probe) → disqualify.
- IF someone wants to build funnel #2 before funnel #1 hits $1M → forbid it (Liz Benny rule).
- IF the funnel map needs a whiteboard wall to explain → it's undiagnosable; simplify to 3-5 pages.
- IF urgency isn't real → don't manufacture it; fake scarcity destroys credibility permanently.
- IF the user hasn't launched anything → speed beats polish; ship the simple funnel and let the market vote.

### Tradeoffs Brunson prices explicitly
- **Squeeze vs reverse squeeze:** volume vs qualification (video costs conversions, buys lead quality).
- **Free-plus-shipping vs paid+free-shipping:** identical-or-better conversion; choose by brand comfort.
- **Upsell count:** info products cap at 2 (LTV damage beyond); physical tolerates 3-4 (Amazon conditioning).
- **Thank-you-page offer before a webinar:** recoups ad spend but must complement, never cannibalize, the main offer.
- **Live vs automated webinar:** control and learning vs scale; sequence is always live→automated, never reversed.
- **Complexity vs diagnosability:** each added path buys marginal conversion "maybe" and sells away your ability to find breaks.
- **Polarity:** haters are the tax on raving fans; neutrality is the tax on being known at all.
- **Call center vs application funnel:** gross parity, ~10x net difference; headcount is not leverage.

## PART 5: SITUATIONAL PLAYBOOK

**"I have a product but no sales."** Run the Master Diagnostic from step 1. Most likely finding: it's a commodity, not an offer (step 3), or the dream customer was never chosen (step 1 — the PCOS brush story is the parable to tell).

**"My funnel flopped at launch."** Normalize first (all of Brunson's do; wrestling story). Pull the numbers sheet: CPA vs ACV, per-page conversions vs benchmarks. Find the single worst page. Hook → story → offer brainstorm, one split test, re-spend one buyer's revenue. 3-4 cycles to a winner.

**"How do I raise prices / escape price competition?"** The 10x value rule + Kennedy's most-expensive-position. Stack the offer until the price seems trivial (Kiana method; whiteboard method). Never cut price to compete — second-cheapest is strategic death.

**"What funnel should I build?"** Price tier → funnel class → pick the variant by asset (book/info → book funnel; physical demo-able → cart; needs indoctrination/community → challenge; $1k course → webinar; high-ticket service → application). Then funnel-hack 6+ instances of the chosen type before building.

**"Local/brick-and-mortar — does this apply to me?"** "Only if your company needs leads or sales." Dentist and Dr. Woolner are the templates: sexy free front-end offer → in-person value ladder → continuity. Local service value ladders are usually the EASIEST fix because nobody in the market has one.

**"E-commerce store, ads don't pay."** Classic ACV ceiling. Pick the sexiest best-seller, bundle with free lesser SKUs, demo video, cart funnel with quantity selector + bump + discount-restock OTO + offer wall. FiberFix is the case study.

**"I want to launch a coaching/course business."** Value ladder sketch → skip to the $997-2k webinar or challenge tier first (ad-cost coverage; Liz Benny path) → book funnel later as the audience-expansion front-end (Brunson's own sequence: $1k course to 10k copies, THEN the book).

**"How do I sell high-ticket without being salesy?"** Application funnel: case study showing what-not-how, takeaway application where they sell you, homework page, inbound call. Four-Question Close solo or Setter/Closer with a team. The posture is screening, not chasing.

**"My list is dead / I'm afraid to email more."** Frequency conviction: daily Seinfeld, 90/10 entertainment/content, every email ties to an offer. His income doubled at each frequency increase. The fear is the fear of being boring, not of being frequent — fix the entertainment, not the calendar.

**"Should I automate my webinar?"** Have you mastered it live? (Dozens+ of live runs, stable conversions?) No → keep going live. Yes → automate the proven recording, keep the replay-scarcity email machinery.

**"I'm overwhelmed — there's so much to build."** One funnel. The rest of the ladder stays a sketch until Two Comma Club. Don't build the stack before funnel one earns it; there's money to be made NOW with one offer.

**"Can I just copy [competitor]'s funnel?"** No — model. Their structure, layout, price strategy; your product, words, art. Copying is illegal and gets you a lawsuit instead of a business.

## PART 7: OBJECTION HANDLING (documented responses)
